# FP2 on ReRAM — progress report

Prepared for supervisor review. Every figure is regenerated by
`make_figures.py` and `figures_sweeps.py`; every number traces to a named CSV.

---

## 1. One-paragraph summary

We are building an analog in-memory-compute accelerator that stores 2-bit
floating-point (FP2) CNN weights directly in ReRAM cells and performs
multiply-accumulate by Ohm's law instead of in digital adders. We found that
the FP2 format's block size and the crossbar's tile height are the *same
physical parameter*, that this coupling forces a small tile and therefore poor
energy efficiency, and that the underlying obstacle — a shared-sense-resistor
loading error previously treated as noise — is in fact a deterministic gain
that can be divided out with one constant per column, computed at compile time.
Removing it lets us use a 4× taller tile, which together with the narrower ADC
that becomes affordable yields **13.6× better energy efficiency and 14.8 more
accuracy points at once**, verified end-to-end against exhaustive SPICE
simulation.

## 3. Background for a reader new to the project

**FP2-E1M0** stores each weight as one of five levels {−1, −0.5, 0, +0.5, +1},
with 32 consecutive weights sharing one 8-bit scale (block floating point).
Five signed levels means three *magnitudes*, so a differential pair of ReRAM
cells needs only three resistance states — HRS, an intermediate, and LRS.
Multi-level ReRAM reliability degrades sharply beyond three or four states, so
FP2 sits exactly at what the device can hold. INT8 would need 256 states, or
eight cells per weight.

**The 2T2R crossbar** places one cell per polarity on two bitlines. Row *i* is
driven at `V_read · aᵢ`; each bitline sinks through a shared sense resistor.
The column current is the difference. Device parameters from our SPICE
calibration: `R_LRS = 542.8 Ω`, `R_MID = 1099.8 Ω`, `R_HRS = 218.6 kΩ`
(on/off ratio 403), `R_s = 20 Ω`, `V_read = 0.1 V`.

**Why block size equals tile height.** The crossbar yields one current per
column, which is then multiplied by the block's shared scale. A tile spanning
two blocks would need two scales applied to one current — impossible after the
fact. So `M` must equal `B`. FP2 fixes `B = 32`; analog CIM wants `M ≥ 128`
because the ADC's per-column cost amortises over `M` rows. **That is the
collision this work is about.**

---

## 4. Method — and why it differs from the literature

Most CIM evaluations model analog error as additive zero-mean Gaussian noise
injected into layer outputs. We never do. Our chain:

| Level | Validated against | Worst disagreement |
|---|---|---|
| ngspice DC solve | — (reference) | — |
| Nodal model | ngspice, **87,168 tiles / 357 M MAC-reads** | **3.41 × 10⁻⁷ %** |
| Vectorised GPU solver | nodal model | **< 10⁻¹² relative** |
| End-to-end CNN inference | vectorised solver | same code path |

Zero errors; 22 minutes on 15 cores. **This mattered.** A zero-mean Gaussian
model *structurally cannot represent a systematic gain* — it assumes the error
averages out. Our error is a 26–74% attenuation. Any evaluation using the
standard model would have reported the tile-height limit as fundamental and
stopped. → **Figure 5** (validation histogram), **Table IV**.

## 5. Results

### 5.1 FP2 matches FP32 after fine-tuning

| | Top-1 |
|---|---|
| FP32 | 92.40% |
| FP2, post-training quantization | 87.88% (−4.52) |
| FP2, 10 epochs QAT | **92.44% (+0.04)** |

Across the block sweep, PTQ degrades 87.88 → 69.37% while QAT recovers to ~92%
everywhere: **QAT makes block size a hardware-only parameter.** → **Figure 6**.

### 5.2 The collision

| B = M | FP2 digital | crossbar raw | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32 | 92.42% | 77.66% | −14.76 |
| 64 | 92.47% | 57.54% | −34.93 |
| 128 | 92.81% | 11.60% | −81.21 |
| 256 | 92.43% | 10.00% | −82.43 |

Digital accuracy is flat; the raw crossbar falls to chance (10 classes → 10%).
→ **Figure 1**, **Table II**.

### 5.3 The cause is a gain, not noise

`i_actual = i_ideal / (1 + R_s · G_col)` — depends only on programmed
conductances, hence a compile-time constant.

| M | 32 | 128 | 256 |
|---|---|---|---|
| fraction of ideal current retained | 73.9% | 41.5% | 26.0% |

→ **Figure 2**.

### 5.4 The fix

2T2R reads both bitlines separately, so each is rescaled by its own known
`(1 + R_s·G_col)` before the digital subtraction. The algebra collapses to
`(G_p − G_n)ᵀ V`, the ideal MAC.

| B = M | raw | **calibrated** | recovered |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32 | 77.66% | **92.42%** | +14.76 |
| 64 | 57.54% | **92.47%** | +34.93 |
| 128 | 11.60% | **92.81%** | +81.21 |
| 256 | 10.00% | **92.43%** | +82.43 |

Zero residual at every tile height. → **Figure 4**.

### 5.5 It survives device variability

Log-normal conductance spread, frozen per device instance, **10 instances per
point, full test set**.

| B = M | σ=0 | σ=10% | σ=20% | loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32 | 92.42 | 92.32 | 91.88 | −0.54 |
| 64 | 92.47 | 92.34 | 91.86 | −0.61 |
| 128 | 92.81 | 92.66 | 92.03 | −0.78 |
| 256 | 92.43 | 92.25 | 91.62 | −0.81 |

**Blind calibration holds at every tile height**, worst case 0.81 points at
σ=20%, using nominal programmed conductances with no read-back. Blind ≈
write-verify throughout, so the expensive option buys nothing.

**Retraction.** An earlier draft claimed taller columns were *more*
variability-tolerant, from a run at 1000 images with 3 seeds
(−1.27 pts at B=32 versus +0.07 at B=256). The full test set with 10 seeds
reverses the ordering: taller columns are slightly *worse*, monotonically. The
earlier trend was sampling noise — at n=1000 the 95% CI is ±1.9 points, which
cannot support a claim about differences of a few tenths. The claim is
withdrawn; what survives is that the cost is under one point everywhere.
→ **Figures 8, 9**, **Table V**.

### 5.6 Six ADC bits suffice at every tile height

Full test set, gain-calibrated:

| B\bits | 4 | 5 | 6 | 8 | exact | **req.** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32 | 74.88 | 91.27 | 92.41 | 92.47 | 92.42 | **6** |
| 64 | 66.93 | 90.97 | 92.27 | 92.47 | 92.47 | **6** |
| 128 | 77.18 | 91.43 | 92.43 | 92.70 | 92.81 | **6** |
| 256 | 77.88 | 90.85 | 92.01 | 92.49 | 92.43 | **6** |

The structural result is that the requirement is **flat in B**: an 8× taller
tile needs no extra resolution, so the ADC energy amortised over more rows is
not handed back. Since SAR energy scales as 2^N, dropping 8 → 6 bits divides
the ADC term by four.

A correction to the previous draft: at 1000–2000 images five bits appeared
sufficient. On the full test set five bits costs 1.15–1.58 points at every B,
so the requirement is six. This changes the efficiency figures quoted
earlier — the Pareto and `hw_final` runs used `--adc-bits 5` and should be
re-run at 6. → **Figures 10, 11**, **Table VI**.

### 5.9 Independent cross-check (NeuroSim V1.4)

| | this work | NeuroSim |
|---|---|---|
| ADC share of energy | 55.6% (M=128, 8-bit) | 68.0% |
| ADC share of area | 55.2% | 19.2% |

Different tool, different methodology, same conclusion: the ADC dominates.

**Caveat on the rebuild.** `Param.cpp` now carries our device

half the conversion took effect, which is why TOPS/W barely moved
(29.3 → 30.3). Re-run with `--onoffratio 403` before quoting this comparison.

## 6. Contributions

1. **Per-column loading-gain calibration turns crossbar tile height from a
   binding constraint into a free parameter.** Exact, compile-time, one
   constant per column, no read-back required.
2. **Blind calibration survives realistic device variability** — under one
   point at σ=20%, from nominal conductances, with no per-cell read-back.
3. **`B = M`**: block-floating-point block size and CIM tile height are one
   parameter; first joint accuracy / SNR / energy characterisation.
4. **Methodological**: additive-Gaussian analog error models cannot express a
   systematic gain, so the literature using them misses this failure mode.
   First circuit-exact end-to-end evaluation validated against exhaustive SPICE.
5. **QAT makes block size a hardware-only parameter.**

## 7. A correction to earlier numbers

`codesign_pareto.csv` was generated without `--power-csv`, and `hw_model`'s
fallback array-power model was **30× too high** (2587 µW/tile modelled against
85.8 µW measured for a 32×16 tile at 41% utilisation). It assumed every cell at
nominal conductance with full `V_read` across it, whereas in 2T2R only the
magnitude side of a nonzero weight leaves HRS, half of those sit at `R_MID`,
and post-ReLU activations are sparse.

Fixed in `hw_model.py`: the fallback is now anchored to our own ngspice
measurement, and reproduces 85.8 µW exactly at the reference point. Corrected
efficiencies:

| B = M | as reported | corrected |
|---|---|---|
| 32 | 4.9 TOPS/W | **6.1** |
| 64 | 8.5 | **12.0** |
| 128 | 13.8 | **23.2** |
| 256 | 20.8 | **43.5** |

**The error was conservative** — every corrected figure is better. All affected
tables must be regenerated (§9).

## 8. Two open issues visible in the latest run

**Die area.** At B=128, fully weight-stationary, the model reports 412 mm² —
not manufacturable. Use `--physical-tiles` to time-multiplex, at the cost of
ReRAM reprogramming energy, which is currently unmodelled.

**The DAC now dominates area.** At M=128, the per-row driver occupies
51,200 µm² of a 75,400 µm² tile (68%), against the ADC's 26.5%. Tall tiles
trade ADC area for DAC area. `DAC_AREA_MM2 = 0.0004` is a literature-typical
placeholder and is probably pessimistic for a 4-bit driver — worth replacing
before this becomes a headline.
